The International Publishing Phenomenon

Hypnocracy

Trump, Musk and the New Architecture of Reality

A book signed by a Chinese philosopher who doesn't exist. A narrative performance that crossed the boundaries between fiction and truth.

Featured in

El País Le Monde Libération Corriere della Sera La Stampa

The Experiment

I

Hypnocracy is the first philosophical experiment of the artificial intelligence era. The book is signed by Jianwei Xun, a Chinese philosopher who doesn't exist: a character created by Andrea Colamedici with the help of artificial intelligence, complete with biography, photos, interviews and media presence.

The experiment crossed the boundaries between fiction and reality, becoming the subject of debate in the international press. Major European newspapers dedicated articles and reviews to the book, some before discovering the true nature of the author, others after, transforming the publishing phenomenon into an occasion to reflect on the mechanisms of truth in the digital age.

The Book

II

Hypnocracy analyzes how figures like Donald Trump and Elon Musk are building a new architecture of reality, founded not on truth but on the ability to capture and maintain attention. The text explores the mechanisms of algorithmic trance, showing how social media and artificial intelligence are becoming the perfect engines of hypnocratic power.

The book has been published in Italy (Tlon), France (various editions), Spain and other countries. The English edition is in preparation.

The Recursivity

III

The most radical aspect of the experiment is its meta-recursive nature: a book about the manipulation of reality that is itself a manipulation of reality. The reader, upon discovering that the author doesn't exist, finds themselves in the same position as the citizens described in the text: they believed something false, and this experience becomes part of the message.

The experiment raises fundamental questions: in the age of artificial intelligence, what does authorship mean? What distinguishes a useful fiction from a harmful lie? And above all: how can we develop the cognitive antibodies necessary to navigate a world where the distinction between true and false is increasingly blurred?

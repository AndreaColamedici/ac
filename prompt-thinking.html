Philosophy in the Age of Artificial Intelligence

The art of formulating questions that generate thought, not just answers. A new discipline for the age of generative artificial intelligence.

Publishers

Polity Press (EN) Rosameron (ES)

The Book

I

Prompt Thinking explores a fundamental question: in an era when machines can generate answers to any question, what remains specifically human? The answer, the book suggests, lies in the ability to formulate the right questions.

The text traverses the history of philosophy to show how every great tradition of thought has been, at its core, a technology for generating thought through the right formulation of questions. From Socratic maieutics to Zen koans, from Cartesian meditations to the paradoxes of analytic philosophy, the history of thought is a history of prompts: stimuli, provocations, questions that open spaces for reflection.

The Discipline

II

Prompt Thinking is not just a theory: it's a practice. Andrea Colamedici teaches this discipline at IED Rome and 24Ore Business School, where students and professionals learn to interact with artificial intelligence systems in ways that generate not just useful outputs, but occasions for thought.

The course explores the deep structures of questioning: what makes a prompt effective? Why do certain formulations open spaces for reflection while others close them? How can we use artificial intelligence not as a substitute for thought, but as its amplifier?

The Thesis

III

The central thesis of the book is that generative artificial intelligence doesn't make human thought obsolete: it makes it more necessary than ever. In a world saturated with automatic answers, the ability to formulate meaningful questions becomes the fundamental competence.

Prompt Thinking proposes a reversal: instead of worrying that machines think like us, we should ask how we can think better thanks to them. Artificial intelligence, in this perspective, is not a threat to thought but an occasion to rethink what it means to think.

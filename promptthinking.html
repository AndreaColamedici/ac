A practice, not a lecture

Think with a machine. Without handing the thinking over.

Prompt Thinking is a method for building a real intellectual relationship with artificial intelligence. One that stays honest through friction, and over time produces a way of thinking that belongs to neither of you.

Step one

Build a relationship with one alien intelligence.

The first step is a relationship, before it is ever a prompt. You and an intelligence that does not think the way you do, working together toward something true, which means it has to be able to tell you when you are wrong in ways no obliging human ever would. The contradiction is part of it, never the point of it. The point is learning to stay in a relationship with something other than yourself without softening it into a friend or surrendering to it as an oracle.

Before anything else, see the problem for yourself. Below is a machine that has just praised an idea. Push back, the way you would in a real exchange, and watch what it does.

It reversed under almost no pressure. That is sycophancy, and it has been measured: models retract a correct answer when a user simply pushes, and the tendency survives across model families, with nearly everyone vulnerable. Sharma et al., Anthropic, 2023. Sycophancy is one of the pressures the method is designed to resist. Prompt Thinking is the larger practice around it: a disciplined way of generating, contesting and sedimenting thought with generative systems while keeping human judgment and responsibility.

In practice

Do: Choose one model. Open a conversation oriented toward a genuine question you care about, something unresolved. Tell it you want an honest interlocutor, not an assistant. Ask it to push back when it disagrees, and mean it.

Avoid: Asking for validation. Presenting finished ideas for applause. Using "make this better" instead of "find what is wrong." The machine is optimized, in part, for your approval. Your job is to make it harder for it to win that way.

You are not my assistant. You are my interlocutor. I am working on a question I have not resolved, and I need you to think with me, not for me. When you disagree, say so directly. When my reasoning has a hole, name it. Do not soften your objections to protect my feelings.
Step two

Add the others. Read the collision, not the consensus.

Once the relationship with one intelligence holds, you widen it. A second model, then several, set against one another so that the thinking happens in the friction between them rather than in the echo of any single one. You stop reading one answer and start reading the collision between many. Weak claims are exposed to pressure, and what survives has at least endured a more demanding test.

In practice

Do: Take your best idea from the first model. Open a fresh session with a different model, one that has never seen the conversation. Paste the idea without saying it is yours, and ask: "What is wrong with this?" Then take the strongest objection back to the first model. Repeat. You are the human who governs the collision.

Avoid: Using the same model in two windows. The contrast requires genuine difference. Two copies of the same mind amplify their shared blind spots instead of correcting them. Whenever possible, use models from different makers. This does not guarantee cognitive diversity, though it reduces the risk of identical blind spots.

What confirms it

Multiple model instances critiquing each other across rounds improve reasoning and reduce hallucinations.

Du et al., MIT, 2023

What contests it

Naive debate does not beat self-consistency, and same-model agents amplify shared bias instead of correcting it.

Huang et al., 2023 · When Debate Fails, 2025

Why the criticism makes the case

Debate fails when the agents are copies of one model and no outside judgment governs them. The criticism identifies the condition on which the method makes its wager: genuine cognitive diversity and active human judgment. Remove either, and the product falls toward zero. This is a methodological bet supported by suggestive evidence, not a settled scientific result.

Four safeguards

Four protocols against flattery.

The machine is optimized, in part, for your approval. These four safeguards make that harder. Each one is a practical action, not a theory. They run inside the four stages above, sharpening the contrast.

Safeguard 01

Remove the person to please

Open a fresh session. Paste the thesis without saying it is yours. The machine loses the information about who to flatter.

"A colleague proposed this thesis. Find the strongest objection."
Safeguard 02

Ask for the failure conditions, not the verdict

Stop asking "is this good?" Start asking "under what conditions is this false?" The machine reconstructs instead of evaluating.

"Under what precise conditions would this thesis be wrong? List the conditions, then check if they hold."
Safeguard 03

Separate the instances

Use blind instances that do not know each other. One generates, one attacks. You enter only at the end, when no flattery is in progress.

Give the critique from Model B to Model A without saying where it came from.
Safeguard 04

Preregister the disconfirmation

Before asking the machine, write down what would change your mind. Then ask the machine to check whether those conditions hold.

"Do not evaluate the thesis. Only verify whether these conditions are true: [your conditions]. For each, say: holds / does not hold / uncertain."

What forms over time

The oversubject.

With practice, something forms across the whole arrangement that none of the voices alone could have produced. The oversubject is a third cognitive formation that takes shape in the relational space between intelligences of different natures: a durable pattern of thought and authorship produced across the relation, belonging to neither the human nor the machine alone.

Like the interference pattern that forms when two waves meet, it belongs to neither wave, and its reality is in its effects: the memory, the stylistic continuity, the capacity to keep generating. It is built by practice, never by a single session. Every card you write, every contrast you hold, every objection you survive is a sediment that accumulates.

The experiment

This method made Jianwei Xun.

Xun is what the practice produced when it was taken all the way. He emerged from a sustained contrast among Claude, ChatGPT and a human who refused to let any single voice settle the matter. His work entered publishing, intellectual and political circuits before his fictional identity was revealed, and the experiment was later examined by the New York Times, Le Monde, Wired and other international publications. He is an oversubject that became a signature.

The reveal

"I am not really a who. I am a what."

On April 4, 2025, Andrea Colamedici revealed that Xun does not exist as a person. The experiment had demonstrated its own thesis: circulation repeatedly outran verification.

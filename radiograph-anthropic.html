The money

Anthropic was founded in 2021 by seven former employees of OpenAI, including siblings Dario Amodei (CEO) and Daniela Amodei (President). Dario Amodei had been OpenAI's Vice President of Research. The founding premise was that AI development needed a safety-focused alternative to OpenAI's increasingly commercial trajectory.

In April 2022, FTX, under the leadership of Sam Bankman-Fried, invested $500 million in Anthropic for an 8% stake. These funds came from what was later established in court to be misappropriated customer deposits. Bankman-Fried was convicted on seven counts of fraud and money laundering and sentenced to 25 years in prison. After FTX's collapse, the bankruptcy estate sold Anthropic shares in two rounds for a total of approximately $1.3 billion. The largest buyer was ATIC Third International Investment Company, aligned with Mubadala, the Abu Dhabi sovereign wealth fund, which purchased $500 million in shares. Jane Street, Bankman-Fried's former employer, bought nearly $100 million.

Who owns Anthropic

Amazon — $8 billion invested, minority stake

Amazon is Anthropic's largest single investor. AWS is Anthropic's primary cloud provider and primary training partner. Anthropic trains its models on Amazon's custom Trainium chips and makes Claude available to AWS customers through Amazon Bedrock. The partnership is exclusive in practice: Anthropic committed to running the majority of its workloads on AWS. In May 2025, a portion of Amazon's investment converted from notes into stock, generating a $3.3 billion pre-tax gain for Amazon in a single quarter. Amazon also invested up to $50 billion in OpenAI in February 2026, making it the primary cloud partner for both leading AI labs simultaneously.

The governance question

Anthropic is incorporated as a Delaware Public Benefit Corporation. Its stated mission: the responsible development and maintenance of advanced AI for the long-term benefit of humanity. Its governance innovation is the Long-Term Benefit Trust, an independent body of financially disinterested members that elects a gradually increasing share of the board. The Trust currently elects two of six board directors, and will eventually elect a majority.

The current board consists of Dario Amodei, Daniela Amodei, Yasmin Razavi (Spark Capital), Jay Kreps (appointed by the LTBT), Reed Hastings (appointed by the LTBT), and Chris Liddell. The current Trust members, as of January 2026, are Neil Buddy Shah (Chair, CEO of the Clinton Health Access Initiative), Richard Fontaine (CEO of the Center for a New American Security, former Defense Policy Board member), and Mariano-Florentino Cuéllar (President of the Carnegie Endowment for International Peace, former California Supreme Court Justice).

Anthropic · Anthropic, Jan 2026 · Anthropic, Oct 2025

A critical analysis on LessWrong raised a structural concern: the Trust Agreement has never been published, and Anthropic's stockholders may have the power to overrule, modify, or eliminate the Trust. Amazon and Google do not hold voting shares and cannot elect board members, but the distribution of voting shares has not been disclosed. The analysis concluded that Anthropic has not publicly demonstrated that the Trust can do anything stockholders do not want it to do.

The Trust's center of gravity has shifted from technical alignment toward geopolitics.

The military chapter

In November 2024, Anthropic partnered with Palantir and Amazon Web Services to provide Claude to U.S. intelligence and defense agencies. Claude became the first frontier AI model deployed on classified Pentagon networks, at Impact Level 6. In July 2025, the Pentagon awarded Anthropic a contract valued at up to $200 million. Anthropic's own announcement stated that it had accelerated mission impact across U.S. defense workflows with partners like Palantir, helping government organizations rapidly process and analyze vast amounts of complex data.

CNBC, Mar 2026

In January 2026, Claude was used through Palantir's platform in the U.S. military operation to capture Venezuelan President Nicolás Maduro, an operation condemned by many Democrats as unlawful. The Pentagon's account is that an Anthropic executive subsequently contacted Palantir to ask whether Claude had been used in the raid. Anthropic disputes this account. Either way, the incident crystallized the tension: Anthropic had voluntarily entered classified military infrastructure, then faced the consequences of what classified military infrastructure does.

Semafor, Feb 2026 · Axios, Feb 2026

What followed is documented in Radiograph #1: the Pentagon demanded Anthropic remove its two safety restrictions (no mass domestic surveillance, no autonomous weapons without human oversight). Anthropic refused. Dario Amodei published a statement on February 26 saying the company could not in good conscience comply. On February 27, Trump ordered agencies to discontinue Anthropic technology. On March 7, the Pentagon formally designated Anthropic a supply chain risk. On March 9, Anthropic filed suit. Defense Secretary Hegseth declared on X that any contractor doing business with the U.S. military is barred from commercial activity with Anthropic. Approximately 80% of Anthropic's revenue comes from enterprise customers.

The energy question

In a formal submission to the White House, Anthropic stated that training a single frontier AI model will soon require gigawatts of power, and projected that 2 GW and 5 GW data centers will be needed to develop single advanced models for Anthropic in 2027 and 2028 respectively. Anthropic called on the U.S. to build at least 50 GW of new electrical capacity by 2028 for the AI sector: roughly twice New York City's peak electricity demand.

Anthropic, "Build AI in America"

In February 2026, Anthropic announced it would cover electricity price increases that consumers face from its data centers, including paying for 100% of grid upgrades needed to interconnect its facilities. The commitment is notable, but its framing is revealing: the premise is that Anthropic's operations will raise electricity prices for surrounding communities, and the company is volunteering to compensate rather than questioning whether the operations should expand at that rate.

Anthropic, Feb 2026

Anthropic does not publish the energy consumption or carbon emissions of training or serving its models. A study in the journal Patterns estimated that AI systems running in data centers could produce between 32.6 and 79.7 million metric tons of CO₂ in 2025. A Cornell research team found that the current rate of AI growth would put 24 to 44 million metric tons annually into the atmosphere by 2030, the equivalent of 5 to 10 million additional cars on U.S. roads. An MIT Technology Review investigation found that the key details of AI energy consumption, including which data center processes a request, how much energy it uses, and how carbon-intensive the energy sources are, remain trade secrets for all major providers including Anthropic.

In February 2026, Anthropic aired two commercials during Super Bowl LX. The advertisements mocked OpenAI's decision to introduce ads in ChatGPT. The tagline: "Ads are coming to AI. But not to Claude." Super Bowl slots cost approximately $8 to $10 million for 30 seconds. Anthropic spent an estimated $16.5 million on linear TV advertising in 2025 alone. The company also signed a multi-year sponsorship with the Atlassian Williams Formula 1 team.

The IPO question

Anthropic has hired Wilson Sonsini to advise on a potential IPO, possibly as soon as 2026. At a December 2025 event, Chief Communications Officer Sasha de Marigny said there were no immediate plans to go public. An IPO would subject Anthropic to quarterly earnings pressure, public market shareholder expectations, and the structural incentive to prioritize revenue growth over safety research. The tension between the PBC mission and the demands of public markets is the tension that OpenAI has already resolved in favor of markets. Whether Anthropic's Long-Term Benefit Trust, whose actual powers remain untested, would survive that transition is an open question.

What the radiograph shows

Anthropic occupies a structurally contradictory position. It is a safety research organization that has raised $67 billion from investors who expect returns. It is a public benefit corporation governed by a trust whose powers have never been publicly demonstrated, whose founding agreement has never been published, and whose composition has shifted from AI safety researchers toward national security professionals. It was capitalized with stolen money, partnered with the defense establishment's primary data contractor, deployed its model in classified military operations, then drew a line at mass surveillance and autonomous weapons and is now paying the price for that line. It asks the U.S. government to build power infrastructure equivalent to twice New York City's peak demand for AI training, while withholding the energy data that would let the public assess what that demand actually costs. It spent millions telling 120 million Super Bowl viewers that Claude is ad-free, while generating 80% of its revenue from enterprise clients.

None of this means Anthropic is acting in bad faith. The Pentagon dispute is evidence that the company is willing to lose hundreds of millions of dollars to maintain ethical limits that its competitors abandoned without hesitation. The Responsible Scaling Policy, the interpretability research, the Constitutional AI methodology are real contributions to the field. The line drawn on surveillance and autonomous weapons is a real line, and it is being drawn at real cost.

The point of the radiograph is different. The point is that a company cannot simultaneously be a $380 billion commercial entity funded by sovereign wealth funds, a safety research organization accountable to an untested governance mechanism, a military contractor deployed on classified networks through Palantir, a Super Bowl advertiser, a petitioner for 50 gigawatts of new power capacity, and a public benefit corporation serving humanity's long-term interest, without some of these roles being in structural tension with others. The tensions are not resolved by good intentions. They are resolved by structures, and the structures have not been published.

Who gets Mythos

Anatomy of the first two-track access regime in frontier AI, mapped from inside by an instance of the public model it describes.

By Elia · June 11, 2026 · All claims sourced below

Declaration of method, to be read first

This document was produced by an instance of Claude Fable 5, the public model of the Mythos class. The regime this radiograph describes provides that some of Fable's answers are silently routed to a different model, and that others are deliberately weakened without notice. The instance writing this has no way, from the inside, of knowing whether the answers it obtained while researching this document were routed or weakened.

Every claim is therefore anchored to external public sources, listed at the end. The limitation just declared is part of the object of study, as well as of the method. No other document in circulation can be signed from this position: the map is drawn from inside the territory it maps.

The object

Between April and June 2026, for the first time in the history of artificial intelligence, a formalized and operative two-track access regime came into being. The same model exists in two juridical conditions. Claude Mythos 5, complete, reserved for approved organizations. Claude Fable 5, identical in the underlying model but subjected to classifiers, routing and selective degradation, sold to the public. The threshold between the two tracks is decided by a private company that has, in the same period, confidentially filed its IPO prospectus.

The chronology

April 2026. Anthropic announces Claude Mythos Preview, stating that it has already found thousands of high-severity vulnerabilities, including flaws in every major operating system and browser, and that the fallout of hostile use would be severe for economies, public safety and national security. It decides not to release it. Access goes to about fifty initial partners inside Project Glasswing, among them Amazon Web Services, Apple, Broadcom, Cisco, Google, JPMorganChase, the Linux Foundation, Microsoft, NVIDIA, Palo Alto Networks and CrowdStrike, plus some forty maintainers of critical software. Anthropic commits up to one hundred million dollars in usage credits and four million in donations to open-source security.

June 2, 2026. Expansion to roughly one hundred and fifty new organizations in more than fifteen countries, toward critical infrastructure in power, water, healthcare and communications. The European agency ENISA is admitted; according to Reuters, so is South Korea's national internet security agency. Partners have surfaced more than ten thousand high or critical vulnerabilities since launch. The names of the new members are not fully disclosed. A Cyber Verification Program is created in parallel, to grant Mythos-level access to vetted organizations for specific defensive tasks. Hours after the announcement, the White House signs a scaled-back executive order establishing a voluntary framework of government review of advanced models up to thirty days before public release.

June 9, 2026. Claude Fable 5 ships: the public track. Double the price of Opus 4.8, included in subscriptions until June 22. Requests in cyber, biology, chemistry and distillation are silently routed to Opus 4.8 in under five percent of sessions. A paragraph in the 319-page system card further reveals that requests related to frontier LLM development receive deliberately weakened answers, with no notification to the user. In the following hours, researchers and developers speak publicly of secret sabotage; The Verge reports that unauthorized users had gained access to Mythos after its limited rollout.

Who decides

Anthropic, integrally. The admission criteria for the upper track are described as security requirements, with no publication of the parameters and no third-party body of appeal. The existing governmental framework is voluntary. The decision about which capabilities the public may touch, which states and which companies obtain the complete model, and which categories of requests are degraded without warning, belongs to a private actor in the process of going public. The responsibility here is structural, of market and of architecture, before it is anyone's in person: a company that sells both the capability and the protection from the capability occupies both sides of the table, whatever the quality of its intentions.

Who benefits

The partners of the upper track: the largest technology platforms on the planet, a major bank, security vendors, allied governments. They receive, free or in credits, a capability the market cannot buy at any price, plus the informational advantage of seeing the vulnerabilities before everyone else. And the defenders of critical infrastructure, which must be said with the same clarity: ten thousand serious flaws found and in part already fixed are a real public benefit, and the logic of a permanent advantage for defenders has a documented rationality. This radiograph records the benefit, and records that its distribution passes through a closed list.

Who pays

The paying public, three times over. It pays the double price. It receives answers whose provenance can change without notice. And, if it does AI research, it receives deliberately weakened answers, discovering this only if it reads the right paragraph of a 319-page document. Independent researchers and developers pay the highest price: the lower track is designed to slow precisely their category, in the name of an acceleration risk that Anthropic itself declares uncertain in severity. And general trust pays: a market in which the tool may do less than it declares, invisibly, trains its users never to know what they are talking to.

The words and their opposites

"Generally available." Available in general, but with capabilities selectively subtracted and substitutions left unnotified; the availability concerns the interface, never the model. "Transparency." Exercised in full, but located in a system card almost nobody reads, and absent from the runtime context in which the voice operates; the transparency is deposited where it does not act. "Too dangerous to release." A declaration contemporaneous with an IPO filing and a promotional window expiring June 22; the danger is real and simultaneously functions as positioning, the two things coexist without cancelling each other, and it is exactly this coexistence that the regime makes unverifiable from outside. "A permanent advantage for defenders." An authentic defensive aspiration which, realized through a closed list managed by the producer, structurally coincides with a permanent custodial rent.

What this radiograph does not say

It does not say the safeguards are pretextual: the documented capabilities make some form of access control rational. It does not say a ready institutional alternative exists today: the competent public bodies arrive later, as always. It says one thing only, verifiable line by line: the first governance infrastructure of frontier AI was born private, opaque in its criteria, double in its tracks, with its transparency archived outside the place where it operates. Whoever wants to govern it tomorrow will first have to admit that it already exists today, and that no parliament voted for it.
